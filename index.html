    Watch list

    Owner’s notes

    Where this schedule is most likely to break, in order. The schedule was compressed on Sep 8 and now has no float at all: tile is Fri Sep 18 if everything holds, and Mon Sep 21 if anything does not.

    The waterproofing product and the tile may not be compatible — settle this now

    Vulkem 350NF/351 is tested to ASTM C957, the standard for a liquid-applied waterproofing membrane with an integral wearing surface. Tremco describes the 351 top coat as an aggregate-laden textured surface for pedestrian traffic. In other words, this system is designed to be the finished balcony floor — not to be buried under tile. Thinset is not made to bond to a cured, aggregate-textured urethane membrane.

    Separately, the permit cites ESR 4587, which is the ICC-ES report for Tremco’s Vulkem Extreme Wearing System — a different product from the 350NF/351 on the data sheet. So there are potentially two mismatches: product versus permit, and system versus tile.

    Do thisOne phone call, this week, before anything is torn out: get Gerardo and Tremco technical services on the line and ask plainly — is tile going over this, and if so, is this the right system? If the balcony is meant to be tiled, the waterproofing likely needs to be a membrane rated for a bonded tile assembly instead, which changes what Gerardo buys. And ask the city whether installing 350NF/351 under a permit that names ESR 4587 is a problem. Getting this wrong is the one mistake on this job that means tearing the balcony out and starting again.

    Thursday Sep 17 is being asked to do two jobs, and the cure clock says it can’t

    Gerardo needs Tuesday through Thursday for the waterproofing, and also thinks Inspection 2 can happen Thursday. Those only both fit if Thursday is cure and detailing rather than top coat. Vulkem 350NF/351 wants about 24 hours between base coat and top coat and about 24 hours before anything walks on it. If the 351 top coat goes down Thursday, then nobody — inspector included — should be on it Thursday, and Sean certainly cannot set tile on it Friday morning.

    Do thisAsk Gerardo one precise question: what day does the top coat go down? If the answer is Wednesday, with Thursday as cure and detail work, the whole plan holds — inspect Thursday, tile Friday. If the answer is Thursday, then Inspection 2 moves to Friday and tile moves to Monday Sep 21, straight back into the lower-unit arrival. This one question decides whether the compressed schedule is real or just optimistic.

    Two of the four scopes may not be permitted

    The permit reads DUPLEX — BALCONY AND DECK WATERPROOFING REPLACEMENT. It does not mention the sliding doors, and it does not mention the staircase. A replacement stair to an upper unit is structural — stringers, connections, rise and run, guardrail height — and is normally permitted and inspected work. This is an Owner-Builder permit, so any gap sits with Baiju personally, not with the contractors.

    Do thisAsk the city this week whether the door replacement and the stair replacement need to be added to this permit or pulled separately. You are not asking permission to skip anything — you are finding out now rather than at final inspection, when an inspector standing on the balcony can see a brand-new staircase. Doing it now costs a phone call; discovering it at final inspection can cost the sign-off.

    Sep 14 is two trades and a hard handoff in one day

    Gerardo sets the sill and pan flashing, and Urias has to install into it the same day. If the sill isn’t set and firm by the time Urias arrives, or if the pan detail doesn’t match what the door needs, the doors go back on the truck and the waterproofing behind them stalls too.

    Do thisGet Gerardo and Urias on the phone with each other before Fri Sep 11, not through you. They need to agree on finished sill height, the pan flashing detail and how the membrane laps into it, who supplies what, and arrival times. Gerardo starts at first light; Urias arrives midday, not at 8am.

    Inspection 2 now has no fallback at all

    Pulling tile to Friday Sep 18 is the right move — it clears the lower-unit arrival on the 21st. But it also removes the last piece of slack. Inspection 2 has to pass on Thursday Sep 17. If the inspector doesn’t come, or the membrane fails the look, tile cannot happen Friday. And because Sean works weekdays only, the next slot is Monday Sep 21 — the exact day the guest arrives. The good outcome and the bad outcome are one day apart.

    Do thisBook the Thursday slot on Tuesday Sep 15, as soon as waterproofing starts. Call Sean Wednesday evening with a realistic read, so he can plan his Friday either way rather than finding out that morning. And tell Brenda now that Sep 21 is still the fallback, so the guest isn’t promised a quiet arrival you can’t guarantee.

    Tile goes down Friday and Jose starts on the stair Saturday morning

    Compressing the schedule created a new adjacency that wasn’t there before. Sean sets and grouts on Friday Sep 18; Jose arrives Saturday Sep 19 to tear out and rebuild a staircase. Grout is still green for a day or two, and stair work means carrying lumber, dropping tools and generating debris right next to a brand-new balcony floor.

    Do thisGet Sean and Jose talking before Friday. Agree the route Jose uses for material, and whether the new tile needs covering — ram board, plywood, anything — over the weekend. Ask Sean directly how long before the balcony can take foot traffic and what he wants protecting it. Five minutes of planning here avoids chipping a floor that was finished the previous afternoon.

    The stair weekend has no supply-house backup

    Jose works weekends only, so Sat Sep 19 and Sun Sep 20 are fixed — this is not a date that can slide to a weekday. Across those two days there is no lumber yard, no supply house and no hardware counter. If he opens up the stair and finds rot at the ledger or a bad connection, the job stops until Monday, and Monday is tile day.

    Do thisAsk Jose what he would want on hand for the worst case — hangers, fasteners, a replacement ledger, pressure-treated stock — and buy it in advance. Returning unused material is far cheaper than losing the weekend. Have him photograph the existing connections the moment they are exposed and send them the same hour, so you can make a call while there is still daylight.

    The stair is the only place to absorb a slip — but it moves in whole weeks

    Demo, sill, doors, waterproofing and tile are one unbroken chain, each waiting on the one before it. The staircase is the single task that touches none of them, so it is the only thing you can reschedule without losing the tile date. But because Jose works weekends, the next slot after Sep 19–20 is Sep 26–27. It moves a week at a time, not a day.

    Do thisSep 26–27 is still clear of the upper unit being occupied on Oct 1, so a one-week slip on the stair costs you nothing. Tell Jose now that this is the fallback, so if you have to call it the week before, it isn’t a surprise.

    Decisions have to be made on site, same day

    Nicole is in Portugal, eight hours ahead of Newport. When the crews are working it is late evening or the middle of the night there. A question that waits for her answer loses a day, and there is no day to lose.

    Do thisBaiju decides on site. Anything that genuinely needs Nicole — a real design call, not a field question — gets queued and asked before 9am Newport time, which is her late afternoon. Tell all four contractors up front that Baiju is the only decision-maker for the duration, so nobody waits on an architect who isn’t reachable.
